**Q1.** Example of viruses having a polyhedral capsid that is with 252 capsomeres is:

A. Adenovirus
B. Tobacco Mosaic Virus
C. Influenza virus
D. Bacteriophage

**Correct Answer: A. Adenovirus**

**Solution:** Adenovirus has an icosahedral (polyhedral) capsid structure composed of 252 capsomeres arranged in a specific geometric pattern. This is a characteristic feature of adenoviruses.

---

**Q2.** The complete, mature & infectious particle of virus is known as:

A. Genome
B. Capsomere
C. Prion
D. Envelope

**Correct Answer: D. Envelope**

**Solution:** The complete infectious viral particle is technically called a "virion." However, among the given options, "envelope" is marked as the answer in the source. A virion consists of the nucleic acid genome surrounded by a protein capsid, and in some viruses, an outer lipid envelope.

---

**Q3.** The causative organism of measles is:

A. Poxvirus
B. Papovavirus
C. Picornovirus
D. Paramyxovirus

**Correct Answer: D. Paramyxovirus**

**Solution:** The measles virus (morbillivirus) belongs to the family Paramyxoviridae. It is an enveloped, single-stranded RNA virus that causes measles in humans.

---

**Q4.** In the life cycle of a bacteriophage, the lysozymes are required in which of the following steps of infection process?

A. Genome injection
B. Penetration
C. Replication
D. Adsorption

**Correct Answer: B. Penetration**

**Solution:** Bacteriophage lysozyme is an enzyme that digests the bacterial cell wall (peptidoglycan layer) during the penetration step. This allows the phage to inject its genetic material into the host bacterium.

---

**Q5.** ______ is transmitted through infected blood and hypodermic syringes.

A. HIV
B. Influenza Virus
C. Morbilli Virus (Measles)
D. Vibrio Cholerae (Cholera)

**Correct Answer: A. HIV**

**Solution:** HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) is transmitted through blood, sexual contact, and contaminated needles/syringes. It is a blood-borne pathogen that can be spread through sharing of infected needles among intravenous drug users or through blood transfusions.

---

**Q6.** In Calvin cycle CO₂ reacts with RuBP to produce:

A. 3-PGA
B. G3P
C. 6-Carbon unstable intermediate
D. 1,3 bisphosphoglycerate

**Correct Answer: C. 6-Carbon unstable intermediate**

**Solution:** In the Calvin cycle, CO₂ combines with ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP) catalyzed by the enzyme Rubisco. This reaction first forms a highly unstable 6-carbon intermediate, which immediately splits into two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA).

---

**Q7.** Which option is correct about a chlorophyll molecule?

A. Chemical formula C₅₅H₇₀O₆N₄Mg
B. Porphyrin ring with nitrogen in center
C. (Methyl) group on second pyrrole ring
D. Aldehyde group on second pyrrole ring

**Correct Answer: A. Chemical formula C₅₅H₇₀O₆N₄Mg**

**Solution:** Chlorophyll has a porphyrin ring with a magnesium ion (Mg²⁺) at the center (not nitrogen). The formula C₅₅H₇₀O₆N₄Mg corresponds to chlorophyll b. Chlorophyll a has a methyl group (-CH₃) on the second pyrrole ring, while chlorophyll b has an aldehyde group (-CHO) at that position.

---

**Q8.** In the journey of electrons from photosystem II to photosystem I plastocyanin is reduced by:

A. Plastoquinone
B. Cytochrome complex
C. Primary electron acceptor of PSI
D. Ferredoxin

**Correct Answer: B. Cytochrome complex**

**Solution:** During the electron transport chain in photosynthesis, electrons move from photosystem II to plastoquinone (PQ), then to the cytochrome b₆f complex, which reduces plastocyanin (PC). Plastocyanin then transfers electrons to photosystem I.

---

**Q9.** Enzyme NADP reductase is responsible for:

A. Reducing NADP⁺
B. Oxidizing NADP⁺
C. Reducing Ferredoxin
D. Reducing P₇₀₀

**Correct Answer: A. Reducing NADP⁺**

**Solution:** Ferredoxin-NADP⁺ reductase (FNR) catalyzes the transfer of electrons from reduced ferredoxin to NADP⁺, reducing it to NADPH. This is the final step of the photosynthetic electron transport chain.

---

**Q10.** The PS II during light reactions receives electrons from splitting:

A. Water
B. Plastoquinone
C. Plastocyanin
D. Ferridoxin

**Correct Answer: A. Water**

**Solution:** Photosystem II receives electrons from the photolysis (splitting) of water. The water-splitting complex (oxygen-evolving complex) associated with PSII splits water molecules into protons (H⁺), electrons (e⁻), and oxygen (O₂).

**Q11.** Mono-saccharides have a general formula represented by:

A. Cₙ(H₂O)ₙ
B. C(H₂O)ₙ
C. C₂(H₂O)ₙ
D. Cⁿ(H₂O)ₙ

**Correct Answer: A. Cₙ(H₂O)ₙ**

**Solution:** Monosaccharides have the general formula (CH₂O)ₙ or Cₙ(H₂O)ₙ, where n is typically 3 to 7. Examples include glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) and fructose (C₆H₁₂O₆).

**Q12.** What is the percentage of H₂O in bone cells?

A. 70%
B. 20%
C. 99%
D. 60%

**Correct Answer: A. 70%**

**Solution:** Most living cells, including bone cells (osteocytes), contain approximately 70% water. Water is essential for cellular functions, providing a medium for biochemical reactions.

---

**Q13.** When glycerol reacts with fatty acid, which type of chemical bond will form?

A. Ester Bond
B. Ether Linkage
C. Hydrogen Bond
D. Ionic Bond

**Correct Answer: A. Ester Bond**

**Solution:** Glycerol reacts with fatty acids through esterification, forming ester bonds. The hydroxyl group (-OH) of glycerol reacts with the carboxyl group (-COOH) of the fatty acid, releasing water (H₂O) and forming an ester linkage.

---

**Q14.** Polysaccharides in plants are synthesized by the process of:

A. Hydrolysis
B. Oxidation
C. Condensation
D. Glycolysis

**Correct Answer: C. Condensation**

**Solution:** Polysaccharides are formed by condensation (dehydration synthesis) reactions. Monosaccharides are joined together through glycosidic bonds, with the removal of a water molecule for each bond formed.

---

**Q15.** Which of the following processes is involved in the breakdown of protein into amino acids?

A. Condensation
B. Hydrolysis
C. Glycolysis
D. Fixation

**Correct Answer: B. Hydrolysis**

**Solution:** Proteins are broken down into amino acids through hydrolysis of peptide bonds. Water is added across the peptide bond, breaking it and releasing individual amino acids. This occurs during digestion.

---

**Q16.** The hydrophilic end of a phospholipid molecule is polar because of the presence of:

A. Glycerol
B. Amine group
C. Fatty Acid
D. Phosphate group

**Correct Answer: D. Phosphate group**

**Solution:** The hydrophilic (water-loving) head of a phospholipid contains a phosphate group, which is polar and charged. This makes it soluble in water and capable of interacting with the aqueous environment.

---

**Q17.** Which monosaccharide will form a gluco pyranose ring in solution?

A. Fructose
B. Glucose
C. Ribose
D. Deoxyribose

**Correct Answer: B. Glucose**

**Solution:** Glucose forms a six-membered ring structure called glucopyranose in aqueous solution. The cyclic form is more stable than the open-chain form. The ring forms when the aldehyde group reacts with the hydroxyl group on carbon 5.

---

**Q18.** Chloroplasts are membrane-bound bodies containing:

A. Enzymes
B. Cisternae
C. Pigment
D. Cristae

**Correct Answer: C. Pigment**

**Solution:** Chloroplasts contain photosynthetic pigments (chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, and carotenoids) embedded in their thylakoid membranes. These pigments absorb light energy for photosynthesis.

---

**Q19.** Which of the following is the function of Golgi Complex?

A. Intracellular digestion
B. Autophagy
C. Autolysis
D. Processing of cell secretions

**Correct Answer: D. Processing of cell secretions**

**Solution:** The Golgi complex (Golgi apparatus) modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids for secretion. It processes molecules received from the endoplasmic reticulum and directs them to their final destinations.

---

**Q20.** The nucleus takes dyes due to the presence of:

A. Chromatin
B. Lipid
C. Metal ions
D. Thylakoids

**Correct Answer: A. Chromatin**

**Solution:** The nucleus is basophilic (stains readily with basic dyes) because it contains chromatin, which consists of DNA and proteins. The negatively charged DNA binds to basic dyes.

---

**Q21.** The organelles only found at the seedling stage in oil seed plants are:

A. Peroxisomes
B. Glyoxisomes
C. Microbodies
D. Vacuoles

**Correct Answer: B. Glyoxisomes**

**Solution:** Glyoxysomes are specialized microbodies found in the cotyledons of oil-rich seeds during germination. They contain enzymes for the glyoxylate cycle, which converts stored lipids into carbohydrates for the growing seedling.

---

**Q22.** Posterior lobe of the pituitary produces:

A. Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH)
B. Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH)
C. Adreno Corticotrophic Hormone (ACH)
D. Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH)

**Correct Answer: A. Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH)**

**Solution:** The posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis) releases antidiuretic hormone (ADH) and oxytocin. These hormones are synthesized in the hypothalamus and transported to the posterior pituitary for storage and release. TSH, ACTH, and FSH are produced by the anterior pituitary.

**Q23.** In human myelinated fibers, nerve impulse travels at ______ meters per second.

A. 100-120
B. 130-150
C. 160-180
D. 190-210

**Correct Answer: A. 100-120**

**Solution:** In myelinated nerve fibers, saltatory conduction allows nerve impulses to travel rapidly, typically at speeds of 100-120 meters per second. Myelination increases conduction velocity by allowing the impulse to jump between nodes of Ranvier.

**Q24.** Nissl's granules are groups of:

A. Mesosomes
B. Lysosomes
C. Ribosomes
D. Chromosome

**Correct Answer: C. Ribosomes**

**Solution:** Nissl's granules (Nissl bodies) are aggregates of rough endoplasmic reticulum and free ribosomes found in neuronal cell bodies and dendrites. They are responsible for protein synthesis in neurons.

---

**Q25.** During the non-conducting state, the neuron membrane is permeable to the efflux of:

A. K⁺
B. Na⁺
C. Ca²⁺
D. Cl⁻

**Correct Answer: A. K⁺**

**Solution:** During the resting (non-conducting) state of a neuron, the membrane is more permeable to potassium ions (K⁺) than to sodium ions (Na⁺). Potassium ions diffuse out through K⁺ leak channels, contributing to the negative resting membrane potential.

**Q26.** Which of the following neurotransmitters is lying outside the central nervous system?

A. Acetylcholine
B. Endorphins
C. Dopamine
D. Gamma-aminobutyric acid

**Correct Answer: A. Acetylcholine**

**Solution:** Acetylcholine is a neurotransmitter found in both the CNS and PNS, but it is the primary neurotransmitter at the neuromuscular junction (outside the CNS). Endorphins, dopamine, and GABA are primarily found in the CNS.

---

**Q27.** Depolarization during the conduction of nerve impulse is due to:

A. Inward movement of Na⁺
B. Inward movement of K⁺
C. Outward movement of K⁺
D. Outward movement of Na⁺

**Correct Answer: A. Inward movement of Na⁺**

**Solution:** Depolarization occurs when voltage-gated sodium channels open, allowing Na⁺ ions to rush into the neuron. This makes the inside of the cell more positive compared to the outside.

**Q28.** Which of the following is NOT a feature of the Autonomic Nervous System?

**Correct Answer: B. Regulate response of skeletal muscles**

**Solution:** The autonomic nervous system controls involuntary functions, regulating visceral organs, glands, and smooth muscles. Skeletal muscles are controlled by the somatic nervous system (voluntary control).

**Q29.** Taste receptor is an example of:

**Q30.** Which of the following belongs to chordates?

**Q31.** Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding the enzyme action?

**Q32.** Induced fit model of enzyme activity suggests that an enzyme:

**Q34.** The change in frequency of alleles at a locus that occurs by chance is known as:

**Q36.** Which of the following increases variation within a gene pool?

B. Crossing over
C. Gene mutation

**Correct Answer: C. Gene mutation**

**Solution:** Gene mutations introduce new alleles into a population, increasing genetic variation within the gene pool. Crossing over and random fusion of gametes redistribute existing variation but do not create new alleles.

**Q38.** The formula calculating the frequency of genotypes and alleles in a population gene pool is known as:

A. Hardy-Weinberg Equation
B. Lamarck Equation
C. Darwin Equation
D. James Hutton Equation

**Correct Answer: A. Hardy-Weinberg Equation**

**Solution:** The Hardy-Weinberg equation (p² + 2pq + q² = 1) describes allele and genotype frequencies in a population at genetic equilibrium under specific conditions (no mutation, migration, selection, etc.).

**Q2.** Frostbites are due to:

a) Fluid retention
b) Vasodilation
c) Muscular expansion

e) Vascular contraction

**Correct Answer: d) Vasoconstriction**

**Solution:** Frostbite occurs when cold temperatures cause vasoconstriction (narrowing of blood vessels), reducing blood flow to extremities (fingers, toes, ears, nose). This leads to tissue damage and cell death due to lack of oxygen and nutrients.

b. Lipids
c. Proteins
d. Vitamins

**Correct Answer: c. Proteins**

**Solution:** Membrane proteins (including channel proteins, carrier proteins, and pumps) are responsible for the selective/differential permeability of the plasma membrane. They allow specific substances to pass through while restricting others.

**Q6.** The following function/activity is NOT controlled by the autonomic nervous system:

a. Cardiac muscles contraction
b. Salivation
c. Smooth muscles contraction
d. Thoughts and emotions

**Correct Answer: d. Thoughts and emotions**

**Solution:** Thoughts and emotions are controlled by higher brain centers (cerebrum, limbic system), not by the autonomic nervous system. The autonomic system controls involuntary functions: heart rate, salivation, smooth muscle contraction, etc.

---

**Q7.** A motor neuron:

a. Carries impulse from effectors to CNS
b. Carries impulse from receptors to CNS
c. Carries impulse from CNS to muscles
d. Connects sensory nerves to ganglions

**Correct Answer: c. Carries impulse from CNS to muscles**

**Solution:** Motor neurons (efferent neurons) transmit nerve impulses from the central nervous system (CNS) to effectors (muscles and glands). Sensory neurons carry impulses from receptors to the CNS.

---

**Q8.** Diffusion of _________ across the post synaptic membrane causes it to depolarise:

a. Calcium ions
b. Chloride ions
c. Potassium ions
d. Sodium ions

**Correct Answer: d. Sodium ions**

**Solution:** When a neurotransmitter binds to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane, ligand-gated sodium channels open, allowing Na⁺ to diffuse into the neuron. This influx of Na⁺ causes depolarization and generates an excitatory postsynaptic potential (EPSP).

---

**Q9.** A reflex action, does not involve the:

a. Brain
b. Motor neuron
c. Sensory neuron
d. Spinal cord

**Correct Answer: a. Brain**

**Solution:** A simple reflex arc (e.g., knee-jerk reflex) involves: receptor → sensory neuron → spinal cord (interneuron) → motor neuron → effector. The brain is not involved, making the reflex rapid and automatic.

---

**Q10.** What happens to the enzyme after an enzyme-catalysed reaction?

a. Reduced to inactive form
b. Becomes inert
c. Changes into substrate
d. Used for another reaction

**Correct Answer: d. Used for another reaction**

**Solution:** Enzymes are not consumed in the reactions they catalyze. They are regenerated at the end of the reaction and can participate in multiple catalytic cycles. This is a key characteristic of catalysts.

**Q11.** Catalase can be activated at pH:

a. 1
b. 3
c. 5
d. 7

**Correct Answer: d. 7**

**Solution:** Catalase, an enzyme that breaks down hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) into water and oxygen, works optimally at near-neutral pH (approximately pH 7). Extreme pH values cause denaturation.

**Q12.** Enzymatic activity can be inhibited by?

a. Heavy metal ions
b. Methane
c. Mutase
d. Noble gases

**Correct Answer: a. Heavy metal ions**

**Solution:** Heavy metal ions (e.g., Hg²⁺, Pb²⁺, Ag⁺) inhibit enzyme activity by binding to sulfhydryl (-SH) groups in the active site or by displacing essential metal cofactors. This leads to enzyme denaturation and inactivation.

**Q13.** A competitive inhibitor:

a. Accelerates the chemical reaction
b. Competes with the enzyme
c. Is irreversible
d. Is reversible

**Correct Answer: d. Is reversible**

**Solution:** Competitive inhibition is reversible. The inhibitor competes with the substrate for the active site of the enzyme. Increasing substrate concentration can overcome competitive inhibition. The inhibitor does not permanently alter the enzyme.

**Q14.** Wings of birds and that of flying lizards provide evidence of:

a. Convergent evolution
b. Divergent evolution
c. No evolution
d. Same origin

**Correct Answer: a. Convergent evolution**

**Solution:** Bird wings and flying lizard (pterosaur) wings are analogous structures that evolved independently (convergent evolution) to serve the same function (flight). They have similar functions but different evolutionary origins.

**Q15.** The embryological stages of ________ show similarity in anatomical features:

**Correct Answer: c. All vertebrates**

**Solution:** Vertebrate embryos (e.g., fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals) show remarkable similarity in early developmental stages, including the presence of pharyngeal arches, a notochord, and a dorsal nerve cord. This suggests common ancestry.

**Q16.** Which enzyme is secreted in the active form?

**Solution:** Amylase is secreted in its active form by the salivary glands and pancreas. In contrast, many proteases (e.g., pepsin, trypsin) are secreted as inactive zymogens (proenzymes) to prevent self-digestion of tissues.

**Q17.** Which hormone stimulates the secretion of gastric juice?

**Q18.** In an inflammatory response, bradykinin causes?

**Q19.** Which of the following is an example of passive immunity?

**Correct Answer: a. Antibodies from mother's milk**

**Q20.** Bacteria that lack flagella are called?

**Q21.** Antibiotics can be used against:

**Q22.** The ______ in semen facilitate the transport of sperms:

**Q24.** The corpus luteum is essentially formed from:

**Q27.** The cells contained in the lacunae of the bone are called:

**Q29.** What happens to calcium when skeletal muscles recover from contraction?

**Q34.** Which is true for an X linked dominant trait?

**Q46.** Most of the neurons in the CNS are?

**Q47.** How do enzymes enable the conversion of substrates into products by?

**Correct Answer: d. Lowering the activation energy**

**Solution:** Enzymes lower the activation energy (Ea) of a reaction, making it easier for the reaction to proceed. They do not change the equilibrium constant; they only increase the rate at which equilibrium is achieved.

**Q48.** According to Lamarckism, the basis of evolution is:
